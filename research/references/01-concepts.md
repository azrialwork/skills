# Research concepts

## The job

Turn a vague question into a deliverable a stranger could trust: every
important claim traceable to a source, every "current" claim stamped with
the date it was verified, every judgment labeled with an explicit
confidence level, and every gap stated instead of hidden.

## What counts as research

Research here means answering a question whose answer is not a single
fact: comparisons, technology evaluations, "is claim X still true",
due diligence, literature reviews. If a plain web search answers it in
one step, that is a lookup, not research -- do not invoke this skill
for lookups.

## Core principles

1. **Source hierarchy first.** Official documentation is the source of
   truth for its own domain. Community mirrors, wikis, and AI-generated
   content farms are never sole sources for an important claim.
2. **Date discipline.** "Current", "latest", and version numbers are
   claims that expire. Stamp them "verified as of <date>" or do not
   make them.
3. **Confidence honesty.** A labeled Medium beats an unlabeled guess.
   Unknowns are findings too -- report them.
4. **Topic flexibility, method rigidity.** The domain changes per task;
   the six phases do not. Declare the domain's source hierarchy up
   front (see `03-topic-adapters.md`) instead of improvising it mid-way.
